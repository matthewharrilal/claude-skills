# Tabs represent verbs, not folders

Tab labels describe what the user does in that section, not the content stored there. Verbs scale across content changes; nouns don't.

## What it means

A verb tab guides intent — it tells the user "this is the place you go to do X." A noun tab forces the user to translate their goal into the app's internal taxonomy. As the product grows, verb tabs absorb new content gracefully; noun tabs require renaming or splitting. Verbs also age better — "Listen Now" still makes sense as the catalog grows.

## Exemplars

- **Apple Music** — "Listen Now" frames the tab as an activity, not a content category
- **Apollo** — "Posts" centers on the act of reading
- **Spotify** — "Search" is purely the verb of looking

## Violators (apps that pay the price)

- **Notion** — "Search / Inbox / [Workspace tree]" mixes verb, noun, and structure, forcing the user to learn three navigation grammars at once

## Why it matters at AAA quality

Verb tabs guide intent. Noun tabs make the user translate goals into your taxonomy, which is the wrong direction of accommodation — the app should accommodate the user's intent, not the other way around.
