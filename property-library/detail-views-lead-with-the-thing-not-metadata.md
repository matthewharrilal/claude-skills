# Detail views lead with the thing, not metadata

Open a photo and see the photo. Open an email and see the email body. Open a song and see the artwork. The user came for the content; surface it first.

## What it means

A detail view is the user's destination — they tapped into it because they wanted to see the thing, not its metadata. AAA apps honor that intent by leading the screen with the content itself and letting chrome, headers, credits, and metadata recede or collapse. The metadata is still accessible (a tap reveals headers, a scroll exposes credits) but it doesn't compete with the content for top billing. Burying the content under a stack of metadata is a betrayal of the user's intent.

## Exemplars

- **Apple Photos** — chrome auto-hides on entry; the photo fills the screen
- **Apple Mail** — the header collapses on scroll, giving the body full primacy
- **Apple Music** — the now-playing surface leads with artwork at full size

## Violators (apps that pay the price)

- **Apps that surface dense metadata above the actual content** — make the user scroll past the chrome to reach what they came for

## Why it matters at AAA quality

Detail views are where the user came to see the thing. Burying it is a betrayal of intent — the screen confirms that the team thought about the data model first and the user experience second.
