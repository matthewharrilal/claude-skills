# One screen, one job

A screen serves one primary intent. If two intents exist, they're two screens — or one screen plus a sheet.

## What it means

A screen with one job has a clear visual answer to the question "what is this?" — the user's eye finds the primary thing first because there is no second thing competing for primacy. A screen with two jobs has no hero, no obvious starting point, and forces the user to read the chrome before they understand what they're looking at. AAA apps push secondary intents into sheets, separate screens, or progressive disclosure rather than letting them share top billing.

## Exemplars

- **Apple Maps** — search and directions are distinct modes, not a single cluttered surface
- **Halide** — the capture screen is one job (take the photo); settings live elsewhere
- **Things 3** — task detail screen does one thing: edit the task

## Violators (apps that pay the price)

- **Notion page screens** — read, edit, navigate, share, and comment on one surface
- **Many dashboard screens** — twelve cards competing for primacy with no clear hero

## Why it matters at AAA quality

Multi-job screens have no hierarchy. The user's eye finds nothing first, and the screen becomes work to parse rather than fluent to use.
