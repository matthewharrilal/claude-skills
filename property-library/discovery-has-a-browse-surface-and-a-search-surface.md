# Discovery has a browse surface and a search surface

Search answers "I know what I want." Browse answers "show me what exists." Both must exist; one is not the other.

## What it means

These are two complementary modes of discovery for two complementary user states. The new user, the exploratory user, and the user with vague intent all need browse — curated, editorial, taxonomic surfaces that show the shape of the catalog. The returning user with a name in mind needs search. Apps that collapse both into one surface (usually a search-only IA) punish the exploratory state and force users to type even when they don't know what they're looking for.

## Exemplars

- **Apple Music** — Listen Now, Browse, and Search are three distinct pillars
- **App Store** — Today and Apps are curated browse; Search is its own pillar
- **Spotify** — Home is the browse surface; Search includes its own browse-by-genre fallback

## Violators (apps that pay the price)

- **Apps with only search** — early Notion forced users to type their way to anything they hadn't authored

## Why it matters at AAA quality

Search-only IA punishes new users and exploratory sessions. Browse-only punishes returning users with intent. AAA apps invest in both because the user's mental state changes session to session.
