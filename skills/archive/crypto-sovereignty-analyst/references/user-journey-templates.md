## Example Templates (Populated)

### Theme: "They acted on early rhetoric, not late-stage proof"

**Source Pattern:** Survivors left when political rhetoric shifted, not when policies were enacted. Einstein didn't wait for confiscation; he read the speeches.

**Historical Behavior:**
- Monitored political rhetoric for demonization patterns
- Acted on "unreasonable" caution before others saw necessity
- Accepted short-term costs (disruption, inconvenience) for optionality

**Crypto Translation:**
- Monitor regulatory rhetoric, enforcement patterns, legislative proposals
- Build exit infrastructure before you need it
- Accept reduced yield/convenience for sovereignty

**Operational Implementation:**

**Today:**
- Set up alerts for regulatory developments in your jurisdiction
- Follow enforcement actions (SEC, DOJ, CFTC in US; equivalent elsewhere)
- Establish self-custody with tested backup/recovery
- Build P2P trading capability (accounts on Bisq, Robosats, local relationships)
- Diversify stablecoin exposure across issuers and jurisdictions

**When Warning Signs Appear (debanking campaigns, explicit self-custody threats, privacy coin bans):**
- Accelerate withdrawal from centralized exchanges
- Move significant holdings to non-blacklistable assets
- Test P2P withdrawal paths with small amounts
- Consider jurisdictional diversification if feasible

**If Already in Crisis (exchange freezes, withdrawal limits, emergency decrees):**
- Use P2P channels already established
- Prioritize assets that can move (BTC, XMR) over those that can be frozen (USDC)
- Accept slippage/cost to preserve capital access

**Trade-off Analysis:**

| Gained | Sacrificed | New Risks |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Optionality if crisis occurs | Yield (CEX staking rewards, etc.) | Self-custody operational risk |
| Non-custodial sovereignty | Convenience of centralized services | Key management burden |
| Privacy preservation | On-chain transparency (for KYC'd holdings) | Legal exposure if privacy = illegal |

**Open Questions:**
- How do you distinguish "unreasonable caution" from "paranoia" in real-time?
- What's the false positive cost of over-preparing?
- Is jurisdictional exit actually viable for most people?

### Theme: "They moved assets gradually and quietly"

**Source Pattern:** Large, sudden asset movements attracted attention. Successful preservation came from patient, systematic diversification over time.

**Historical Behavior:**
- Multiple small transactions over months/years
- Avoided patterns that triggered reporting
- Used multiple channels, not single point of failure

**Crypto Translation:**
- Gradual withdrawal from exchanges
- Multiple wallets with no linking transactions
- Timing distribution to avoid pattern recognition

**Operational Implementation:**

**Today:**
- Establish multiple wallets (hardware + software, different chains)
- Begin regular small withdrawals rather than large single transfers
- Avoid round numbers (withdraw 0.0847 ETH, not 0.1 ETH)
- Use different withdrawal addresses each time
- Consider privacy layer usage for separating positions

**When Warning Signs Appear:**
- Accelerate withdrawal schedule while maintaining pattern discipline
- Prioritize jurisdictionally sensitive assets
- Close positions that can't be withdrawn (locked staking, etc.)

**If Already in Crisis:**
- Speed trumps stealth if exit window closing
- Use whatever channels still function
- Accept surveillance cost if alternative is total loss

**Trade-off Analysis:**

| Gained | Sacrificed | New Risks |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Reduced single-transaction scrutiny | Speed (takes months to fully execute) | Multiple withdrawal points = more operational surface |
| Pattern avoidance | Simplicity | Tracking your own positions becomes complex |
| No single point of failure | Efficiency | More wallets = more key management |

**Open Questions:**
- What does "quiet" mean when blockchain is public by design?
- How effective is timing/amount obfuscation against sophisticated analysis?
- At what point does complexity become its own risk?

### Theme: "They maintained geographic diversity"

**Source Pattern:** Multiple jurisdictions, multiple banking relationships, multiple physical locations for assets.

**Historical Behavior:**
- Foreign bank accounts established before crisis
- Assets physically located in multiple countries
- Personal mobility (second passports, foreign residency)

**Crypto Translation:**
- Multi-chain presence (not all holdings on one chain)
- Infrastructure diversity (not dependent on single RPC, frontend, or jurisdiction)
- Legal structure diversity if significant holdings

**Operational Implementation:**

**Today:**
- Establish presence on multiple chains (BTC, ETH, Solana, Cosmos)
- Use different wallet providers (not all Metamask, not all Ledger)
- Bookmark multiple frontends for critical protocols
- Run or have access to independent RPC
- Consider geographic distribution of multisig signers if applicable

**When Warning Signs Appear:**
- Shift weight toward more censorship-resistant options
- Test alternative access paths (VPN + alternative frontend)
- Reduce exposure to chains/protocols with concentrated jurisdiction

**If Already in Crisis:**
- Route through least-affected chain/jurisdiction
- Accept cross-chain bridge risk if necessary for mobility
- Prioritize asset preservation over optimization

**Trade-off Analysis:**

| Gained | Sacrificed | New Risks |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| No single chain failure mode | Unified portfolio view | Bridge risk when moving cross-chain |
| Infrastructure redundancy | Simplicity | More systems to secure/monitor |
| Jurisdictional optionality | Efficiency | Complexity, potential tax/legal complications |

**Open Questions:**
- Does multi-chain actually help if chains share infrastructure (AWS, Infura)?
- What's the meaningful difference between chains for censorship resistance?
- Is "geographic diversity" meaningful when validators aren't geographically diverse?

### Theme: "They built networks before needing them"

**Source Pattern:** Relationships, knowledge, and capabilities established during calm periods proved essential during crisis.

**Historical Behavior:**
- Foreign bank relationships established years before needed
- Legal and professional networks in place
- Knowledge of exit options already researched

**Crypto Translation:**
- P2P trading relationships and reputation built
- Technical skills developed (can interact with protocols directly)
- Exit paths tested and proven

**Operational Implementation:**

**Today:**
- Complete small P2P trades to build reputation (Bisq, Robosats, HodlHodl)
- Learn to interact with contracts directly (Etherscan, CLI tools)
- Practice using alternative frontends, VPNs, Tor
- Understand your specific jurisdictional legal landscape
- Build relationships with others who share operational concerns

**When Warning Signs Appear:**
- Activate network (are your P2P counterparties still available?)
- Verify technical skills still current (has protocol changed?)
- Confirm exit paths still functional

**If Already in Crisis:**
- Rely on pre-built relationships (can't establish trust during crisis)
- Use practiced technical skills (no time to learn)
- Execute plans already made

**Trade-off Analysis:**

| Gained | Sacrificed | New Risks |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Functional capabilities when needed | Time investment upfront | Reputation/network security risks |
| Technical independence | Convenience of custodial services | Mistakes while learning |
| Tested exit paths | Could have been yield farming instead | Social risk of crypto associations |

**Open Questions:**
- How do you build P2P trust without compromising privacy?
- What's the minimum viable technical competence?
- How do you maintain skills/relationships over years of non-use?
