## Absorption Mechanics -- How to Read Research Without Reducing It to a Checklist

337 findings across 6 files is an overwhelming corpus. The failure mode is NOT failing to read it all. The failure mode is reading it all and extracting a flat list of techniques to apply. That reduces research to a checklist, which is exactly the compression loss this skill exists to prevent.

### Read for Spatial Implication, Not Information

When R3 says "density varies across 6 dimensions," the information is: density has 6 dimensions. The SPATIAL IMPLICATION is: your page's density should vary across THOSE dimensions, not just spatially. When R4 describes a Z-pattern, the information is: readers' eyes move in Z-shapes. The spatial implication is: does THIS content's argument have a moment that demands a Z-shaped reading path?

For every finding, ask: "What does this finding IMPLY about what my page should do?" not "What does this finding tell me to add?"

### Triage Without Premature Dismissal

The pass-8 re-evaluation mandate exists because findings that seem irrelevant at pass 2 become critical at pass 8. The page has changed. Your perception has changed. A finding about "fractal self-similarity" seems abstract when the page has 3 zones. At pass 8, the page has 12 zones with sub-zones, and fractal self-similarity is suddenly the most important principle in the entire corpus.

### Research Transforms from Instruction to Vocabulary

This transformation is the WHOLE POINT of Wave 2:
- **Pass 1:** R4 is "a list of axis patterns to try." You read it, extract an idea, apply it.
- **Pass 5:** R4 is "a framework for thinking about how readers move through space." You consult it when you notice a reading-path problem.
- **Pass 10:** R4 is "a vocabulary for describing reading paths that the content itself suggests." You think in axis language without opening the file.

The equivalent transformation happens for every file. R3 stops being "density rules" and becomes "how I think about pacing." R5 stops being "combination principles" and becomes "how I think about relationships between things."

### Citing vs. Thinking In

The difference between CITING a finding and THINKING IN its vocabulary:
- **Citing:** "I applied R3's density rhythm principle" -- you are treating research as instructions from an authority.
- **Thinking in:** "The section accelerates here -- it needs compression" -- you are using density vocabulary as your own perceptual framework.

The latter means you have absorbed the research. The former means you have merely read it.

### Compound Insights

Compound insights emerge ONLY after multiple files interact in the builder's mind. R3 density + R5 combination produces insights neither contains alone -- "these two components have the same density profile but different relational weight, so they need to be adjacent but asymmetric." No single file contains this insight. It emerges from holding both files' vocabularies simultaneously.

### Track Absorption in the Notebook

Track which findings changed what you SEE, not just what you BUILT. The notebook entry "Applied finding R4-037" is process documentation. The notebook entry "After reading R4-037, I can now see that the content's three-part argument creates a diagonal reading path I was not seeing before" is absorption tracking. The second kind is what matters.

## Diagnostic Override Protocol

After each pass, the agent may override the next pass's primacy:
1. What dimension of the page feels weakest right now?
2. Which research file addresses that dimension?
3. Has that file been at primacy in the last 2 passes? If NO: put it at primacy next. If YES: follow the default schedule.
4. Has any file gone 4+ passes without primacy? If YES: that file gets primacy next, regardless.

**After overriding:** Resume the default schedule at the next sequential position. The overridden file's slot is consumed, not replayed. Example: if the default schedule says R3 at position 6 but you override position 6 with R4, then position 7 picks up where the default schedule says (R1 2nd), not R3.

## Look Before Reading

At the START of Wave 2 (and every subsequent wave), take a screenshot and look at the page BEFORE reading any new research. Ask: "What does the page need?" This question precedes "What does the research offer?" Looking comes first. Research comes second.

This is critical for absorption. If you read research first, you will project research ideas onto the page. If you look first, you will read research with the page's ACTUAL needs as your filter. The page's needs direct your attention within the research corpus. Without looking first, you are shopping in a 337-item catalog. After looking first, you are searching for specific solutions to specific problems you have already identified.

## Findings Triage

On first read of each file, classify findings into:
- **HIGH:** Directly addresses a gap in THIS page. Apply in this pass or next.
- **MEDIUM:** Relevant but not urgent. Will revisit after 3+ more passes.
- **LOW/SKIP:** Not relevant to this content. May revisit if page changes.

Track your triage in the builder's notebook (3-5 lines per file, updated each pass). **At pass 8, re-evaluate ALL deferred and skipped findings.** Your page has changed. Your understanding has changed. At least 3-5 findings you dismissed in passes 1-3 will now be relevant. This is not optional -- premature dismissal is the primary failure mode of research absorption. If you apply 0 findings from any research file, write a notebook entry explaining WHY -- the explanation is the safeguard against unconscious neglect.

### The Pass-8 Re-evaluation Is Not Optional

Why pass 8 specifically? By pass 8, the page has undergone the STRUCTURAL → INTEGRATION transition. Components that did not exist at pass 2 now exist. Relationships that were not visible at pass 3 are now the dominant feature of the page. A finding about "nested containers encoding concept hierarchy" that seemed irrelevant when the page had 3 flat sections becomes urgent when the page has 8 zones with internal structure.

The re-evaluation mandate exists because your perception has changed as much as your page has. You are not the same evaluator you were at pass 2. The findings have not changed -- YOU have.

### Staleness Signatures in Triage

- Zero triage updates across passes -- findings dismissed at pass 2 stay dismissed at pass 12
- All research files getting equal attention regardless of content relevance (some files serve THIS content more than others)
- The 337-finding overwhelm response: reading all files once, extracting a few ideas, and never returning

## The Build-Look-Refine Cycle

**Each pass is a full build-look-refine cycle.** Not "read findings, apply findings." Each pass: read the research → build CSS changes → screenshot and scroll → evaluate with 5 layers → refine → note what you learned. Then start the next pass. The research changes meaning with each cycle because the PAGE has changed.

### How the Cycle Evolves Across 10+ Passes

- **Passes 1-3 (STRUCTURAL):** The cycle is dominated by BUILDING. Read primacy file fully → make bold, rough additions (100-200 lines) → screenshot → notice the big gaps → refine. Reading is broad. Building is ambitious. Evaluation is structural ("do zones exist?").
- **Passes 4-6 (INTEGRATION):** The cycle is dominated by LOOKING. Read primacy file → build relational changes (50-100 lines) → screenshot and REALLY LOOK → notice how components talk to each other → refine connections. Reading is targeted. Building is relational. Evaluation shifts to "do things talk?"
- **Passes 7-9 (DEPTH):** The cycle is dominated by REFINING. Read specific findings → make precise changes (10-40 lines) → screenshot at multiple viewports → evaluate typography, timing, coupling → refine at the character level. Reading is surgical. Building is precise. Evaluation asks "does form serve content meaning?"
- **Passes 10+ (MASTERY):** The cycle is dominated by EVALUATING. The research is internalized -- the agent is no longer "applying R3" but IS density thinking. Scan for system-level insights → make invisible-grain changes → screenshot → evaluate holistically → the evaluation itself produces the understanding that drives the next pass. Evaluation has become the creative act.

## Dimension Visibility at Wave 2

Research does not just add techniques -- it ADDS DIMENSIONS OF PERCEPTION. Before R3, the builder literally could not see temporal density. After R3, it is visible everywhere. This is the deepest function of Wave 2: expanding what the builder is capable of perceiving.

- **Structural** deepens from skeleton to SPATIAL SCAFFOLDING (R4 axis patterns). The skeleton from Wave 1 gains spatial logic -- not just "zones exist" but "zones are arranged to encode reading paths."
- **Temporal** AWAKENS fully -- R3 density dimensions include temporal density (scroll velocity, pacing). Before R3, the builder could set spacing. After R3, the builder can see PACING -- where the page accelerates and decelerates, and whether that pacing tracks the content's velocity.
- **Material** deepens through research -- R1 documentation patterns add material vocabulary. The builder begins to distinguish between institutional weight and open lightness as material qualities, not just visual styles.
- **Chromatic** remains CONSTRAINED by design system but research may suggest semantic extensions. Color is still an exception state, but the builder may now see WHERE color should break the monochrome -- moments the content demands it.
- **Typographic** deepens -- R1 typography hierarchy, R3 density at character level. The builder begins to see typography as VOICE, not style. Different content registers demand different typographic environments.
- **Behavioral** AWAKENS -- R3 cognitive/interaction density, R4 reading paths. The builder can now see that the page is not static -- it has behaviors implied by the content's internal logic.
- **Relational** BEGINS -- R5 combination theory describes how components relate. Before R5, components were independent. After R5, the builder can see that two components argue with each other across distance.
- **Atmospheric** is still NASCENT -- too early for the gestalt, but hints emerge as dimensions accumulate. The builder may notice moments where multiple dimensions converge and produce a feeling. These hints are the seeds of atmospheric coherence in later waves.

The key insight: each research file does not just give you TECHNIQUES. It gives you EYES. R3 gives you density-eyes. R4 gives you axis-eyes. R5 gives you relationship-eyes. Wave 2 is the wave where you acquire most of your perceptual vocabulary. The techniques are secondary to the perceptual expansion.

- **Passes 1-3 (STRUCTURAL):** Evaluation asks "what does this research say I should ADD?" (research as instruction)
- **Passes 4-6 (INTEGRATION):** Evaluation asks "how does this research change how I SEE the relationships between things I already built?" (research as diagnostic lens)

This is the deepening arc of Wave 2: research begins as instruction (someone telling you what to do), becomes a diagnostic lens (a way of seeing problems), becomes a content-deepening tool (a way of understanding what the content means), and finally becomes internalized taste (a way of thinking that no longer requires consulting the source). If you reach pass 10 and are still consulting research files for IDEAS rather than using research VOCABULARY to describe problems you can already see, the absorption has not completed.

## What "10+ Passes Per Slice" Means in Practice

The wave has 12-18 passes total. Each research file gets 2-3 turns at primacy position (read first, highest attention). But "10+ passes per slice" does NOT mean "2-3 primacy turns plus keeping the dimension in mind." It means: the full build → look → refine → enrich cycle, repeated with each file's dimension as an ACTIVE lens, 10+ times. When R3 is not at primacy, the agent is still ACTIVELY applying density thinking at every pass -- not passively "keeping it in mind" but making density-informed CSS changes, screenshotting to check density rhythm, evaluating density at multiple scales, and refining density decisions. Every pass is a full cycle for EVERY active dimension, not just the primacy file. The primacy file gets the deepest read; all other files' dimensions get active cycle engagement.

Track this in your notebook: for each pass, note which dimensions you actively built-from, looked-at, and refined -- not just which file was at primacy. If any dimension goes 3+ passes without an ACTIVE build-look-refine cycle (not just "I thought about it"), that dimension needs focused attention. "10+ meaningful engagements" means 10+ passes where you physically changed CSS, took a screenshot, and evaluated through that dimension's lens.

## Wave-Specific Staleness Signatures

These are the staleness patterns specific to Wave 2. When you detect 2+ of these, you are stale -- apply the staleness recovery protocol from SKILL.md before continuing.

1. **Checklist processing:** Treating all 337 findings as a checklist to process sequentially. Reading file after file, extracting items, ticking them off. The research becomes a to-do list instead of a perceptual expansion.
2. **Surface application:** Applying findings at the surface level ("R4 says use Z-pattern → add a Z-pattern") without spatial implication. The finding was treated as an instruction, not a vocabulary word.
3. **No perceptual change entries:** The notebook shows "applied finding X" entries but never "finding X changed what I see." If research is only producing BUILD actions and never PERCEPTION changes, it is being absorbed mechanically.
4. **Undifferentiated attention:** All research files getting equal attention regardless of content relevance. Some files serve THIS content more than others -- R4's 192 findings are not all equally relevant. Uniform attention is a sign of processing, not absorption.
5. **Pass-1 questions at pass 8+:** Evaluation still asking "what should I add?" instead of "has this research changed how I SEE the content?" The evaluation questions should evolve with the pass count.
6. **The overwhelm response:** Reading all files once, extracting a few ideas, and never returning. This is the most common Wave 2 failure -- the corpus is large enough to trigger a "good enough" response after a single pass through each file.
7. **Frozen triage:** Zero triage updates -- findings dismissed at pass 2 stay dismissed at pass 12. The triage should be a living document that changes as the page changes.
8. **Treating research as instruction at pass 10:** If you are still framing research as "R3 says I should..." at pass 10, you have not absorbed it. By pass 10, you should be thinking "the pacing breaks here" without attributing the perception to a specific finding.

## Cognitive Transformation Gate

Before leaving Wave 2, verify these conditions. They are not pass/fail gates -- they are diagnostic indicators of whether Wave 2 achieved its purpose. If fewer than 4 are met, extend the wave.

**The 3-condition exhaustion model applies here:** (1) Delta stabilized -- research-driven CSS changes produce diminishing returns. (2) Research re-read produces no new spatial ideas -- the builder has absorbed the research, not just read it. (3) The "infinite time" answer is invisible-grain adjustments, not structural additions. These three conditions are the CORE of the gate. The numbered conditions below are diagnostic evidence that the exhaustion model holds.

1. **Vocabulary internalization:** Can the builder describe the page's strengths and weaknesses using research vocabulary WITHOUT referencing specific findings? ("The density breaks in the transition" not "R3 finding 14 says density should vary.") Thinking in vocabulary, not citing.

2. **Perceptual expansion:** Has the research changed what the builder SEES on the page? Can they name at least 3 perceptions that did not exist before this wave? (e.g., "I can now see temporal density -- the pacing of the scroll experience. I can now see axis patterns -- the reading path the content suggests. I can now see relational weight -- how components argue across distance.")

3. **Triage evolution:** Has any finding been promoted from LOW to HIGH on re-evaluation? This is evidence that the builder's perception changed -- findings that seemed irrelevant became relevant because the page changed or the builder changed.

4. **Exhaustion signal:** Can the builder annotate every finding as "applied," "irrelevant to this content," or "addressed indirectly"? Research re-read produces no new spatial ideas. The builder has absorbed the research, not just read it.

5. **Evaluation evolution:** The builder's evaluation questions have shifted from "what should I add?" to "what compound relationships am I not yet seeing?" This shift is the cognitive signature of research internalization.

6. **Compound insight evidence:** The builder can name at least one insight that emerged from the interaction of multiple research files -- an idea that no single file contains but that became obvious when holding multiple files' vocabularies simultaneously.

7. **Minimum pass count:** 12 passes completed. This is anti-compression insurance only -- it ensures the builder spent enough time for absorption to occur. Extend to 18 if files remain under-engaged (any file with fewer than 2 primacy turns, or any dimension that went 3+ passes without active engagement).
