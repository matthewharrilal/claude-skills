# Wave 4: Synthesis + Provenance

**Input:** RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS.md, semantic-rules.md, selected provenance documents.

**Minimum passes:** 8. **Expected passes:** 8-12. **Exhaustion signal:** Conflicts between earlier wave applications resolved. Page is internally consistent.

---

## The Critical Cognitive Shift

Wave 4 is not about adding more to the page. It is about changing how you THINK about the page.

Waves 2-3 gave you a spatial vocabulary -- density, organization, combination, axis, typography, case study patterns. You absorbed findings. You built from them. You deepened dimensions one at a time. But you applied them in relative isolation. Each wave had its own lens, its own primacy files, its own deepening arc. The page grew richer in each dimension separately.

Wave 4 is where you stop asking "what should I add to the page?" and start asking "how should I think about the page differently?" The input files are META-documents -- documents that describe how findings relate to each other. Which conflicts exist. Which principles override. Where your Wave 2 density decisions contradict your Wave 3 organizational decisions. Where compound effects emerge that neither wave could have produced alone.

This is the wave where dimensional INTERACTIONS become the primary lens. At Waves 2-3 you built each dimension. At Wave 4 you evaluate how dimensions TALK TO EACH OTHER.

---

## Absorption Mechanics for Wave 4

The Absorption Protocol applies here as everywhere, but what you are absorbing is qualitatively different. You are not absorbing findings about what to build. You are absorbing frameworks for how to evaluate what you have already built.

### Reading RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS for Conflict Detection

Do not read the synthesis as a summary of what you have already absorbed. Read it for CONFLICT detection between your earlier wave applications. Not theoretical conflicts -- actual CSS decisions that contradict each other. Where did you apply R3's density principle in a way that conflicts with OD-006's organizational principle? Where did your Wave 2 density treatment create a visual effect that your Wave 3 case study application undermined?

The synthesis document describes relationships between research findings. Your page is where those relationships became concrete CSS. The synthesis reveals where your concrete decisions are fighting each other.

### Reading Semantic-Rules for Framework Revision

Semantic-rules is not a checklist of constraints to verify. It is a set of principles that should change how you evaluate EVERYTHING you have built. Read it for framework revision -- for principles that restructure your evaluation, not rules that flag violations.

The question is not "does my page comply with semantic-rules?" The question is: "How does semantic-rules change what I SEE when I look at my page?" If you read the entire file and your evaluation of the page does not change, you read at the wrong level.

### Reading Provenance for Reasoning Chains

Provenance documents reveal the REASONING CHAIN behind conflicting guidelines. Understanding WHY principles conflict makes resolution more than compromise. When you know that a density principle and an organizational principle conflict because they were derived from different content types, you can resolve the conflict by asking which content type YOUR page more closely resembles -- rather than splitting the difference or arbitrarily choosing one.

Select 1-2 provenance documents that address conflicts you have identified between your Wave 2 and Wave 3 applications. These are agent-selected, not prescribed. Choose the provenance that illuminates your specific conflicts.

### Resisting the Synthesis Trap

The synthesis trap: resolving a few specific conflicts without changing the evaluative framework. You find 3 places where density and organization conflict. You resolve each one. You feel done.

But individual conflict resolution is necessary and INSUFFICIENT. The synthesis should change HOW YOU THINK ABOUT density-organization interaction EVERYWHERE -- not just where you found a conflict. If you resolved 3 conflicts but your framework for evaluating density-organization interaction is the same as it was before reading the synthesis, you resolved symptoms, not the underlying evaluative gap.

The test: after resolving specific conflicts, can you articulate how density-organization interaction works everywhere on the page? Not just where you found problems -- EVERYWHERE? If not, the synthesis has not yet changed your framework.

### Arguing Against Your Proudest Decisions

List 3 decisions from Waves 1-3 that you are most proud of. Now argue AGAINST each one using the synthesis principles. If you cannot construct a compelling counter-argument, you either lack synthesis understanding or you are defending decisions out of attachment rather than quality.

This is not an exercise in self-destruction. It is a test of synthesis depth. A builder who has truly absorbed the synthesis can argue both sides of every decision -- because the synthesis reveals the TENSIONS between principles, and every decision is a resolution of tension. If you cannot argue the other side, you have not understood the tension.

## The Fundamental Cycle at Wave 4

Each pass is a full cycle: **Read synthesis/provenance --> build conflict-resolution CSS changes --> screenshot and scroll --> evaluate at meta-level --> refine --> decide what next.** The cycle applies here too -- not just "resolve conflicts," but build-look-refine the resolutions. The cycle has been the fundamental unit since Wave 0 and remains so here. 8-12 passes means 8-12 full build-look-refine cycles with synthesis documents as the active lens. Not 8 conflict-resolution tasks.

## Dimension Visibility at Wave 4

Synthesis reveals dimensional INTERACTIONS. The shift is fundamental: you stop evaluating dimensions in isolation and start evaluating how they compound, conflict, and entangle.

**PRIMARY -- Relational:** Synthesis IS about relationships -- relationships between findings, between dimensions, between your earlier decisions. The relational dimension becomes the dominant lens because Wave 4's cognitive work is relational at its core. How does density interact with organization? How does axis interact with typography? Where do compound effects emerge that neither dimension produces alone? Where do compound effects FAIL -- where two dimensions that should interact are operating in separate zones without cross-talk?

**ALL 8 DIMENSIONS ACTIVE:** By Wave 4, all 8 perceptual dimensions should be visible and evaluable. But the evaluation mode changes. You are no longer building each dimension. You are evaluating how dimensions TALK TO EACH OTHER. Dimensional entanglement becomes the primary lens. Does changing the density in Zone 3 require a corresponding change in typographic rhythm? Does the material quality of the institutional sections interact with the chromatic arc? Where do you see compound effects? Where should compound effects exist but do not?

**CONDENSING -- Atmospheric:** When dimensional interactions are explicitly understood -- not just accidentally present -- the gestalt begins to have intentional shape. The atmospheric dimension at Wave 4 starts condensing from "the emergent totality of everything else" into something you can begin to perceive and evaluate directly. You are not yet BUILDING atmosphere (that is Waves 6-8). But you are beginning to SEE it -- to notice where the interaction of all dimensions creates a feeling, and where that feeling breaks.

## Content Reading Depth at Wave 4

The builder should be solidly in **DEMANDS.**

**What DEMANDS looks like at Wave 4:** "The content DEMANDS internal consistency" -- synthesis reveals where the builder has served individual content demands in contradictory ways. You applied density where the content compressed and organization where the content hierarchized -- but in some zones, those treatments contradict each other. The content DEMANDS that these contradictions be resolved, not because of an abstract principle, but because the reader experiences contradiction as confusion.

**Beginning of FUSES:** When meta-understanding resolves contradictions, the builder starts to experience content and form as inseparable. Not "the content needs X spatial treatment" but "this IS what the content looks like." The DEMANDS-to-FUSES transition is what synthesis makes possible. Without resolving contradictions, form remains a set of local decisions rather than a unified expression. With contradictions resolved, the page begins to feel like a single thing -- not a collection of independently good sections but a unified spatial argument.

The synthesis is what makes the DEMANDS-to-FUSES transition possible. Without it, you have a page of locally excellent sections that do not quite cohere. With it, you begin to experience the page as a single entity whose form IS its content.

## The 5 Layers at Wave 4 Depth

- **L1 (Seeing):** "Is the page internally consistent? Do the findings from Waves 2-3 contradict each other? Where did I apply R3's density principle in a way that conflicts with OD-006's organizational principle?"

- **L2 (Why now):** "The synthesis documents reveal RELATIONSHIPS between findings I applied in isolation. Before reading the synthesis, I saw density and organization as separate dimensions. Now I see they interact -- high density in an organizational hierarchy zone creates a different reading experience than high density in a philosophical zone. This META-understanding changes what I evaluate."

- **L3 (Not seeing):** "By Wave 4, I've been building for 40+ passes. My habituation is DEEP. I've seen this page so many times that things which were jarring at pass 5 now feel normal. The synthesis documents offer a fresh vocabulary for diagnosing problems I've stopped noticing. Am I using that vocabulary, or am I just confirming my existing decisions?"

- **L4 (How to know):** "List 3 decisions from Waves 1-3 that I'm most proud of. Now argue AGAINST each one using the synthesis principles. If I can't construct a compelling counter-argument, I either lack synthesis understanding or I'm defending decisions out of attachment rather than quality."

- **L5 (Evaluation evolving):** "Wave 4 evaluation should be META-evaluation -- evaluating how I evaluated in earlier waves. Did my Wave 2 evaluation miss density-organization interactions? Did my Wave 3 evaluation miss compound combination potential? If I'm evaluating at the same level as Wave 3, I haven't absorbed the synthesis. Evaluation at Wave 4 should be ABOUT evaluation at Waves 1-3."

## Pass-Level Deepening

The evaluation questions change as the wave progresses. This is mandatory deepening, not optional variation.

**Passes 1-3 (Conflict Detection):** "What conflicts exist between my earlier applications?" The builder reads synthesis and provenance documents for the first time with the page as concrete context. Every conflict between Wave 2 and Wave 3 applications should be identified and named. The notebook should list specific CSS decisions that contradict each other, not abstract tensions.

**Passes 4-6 (Meta-Principle Application):** "How do the resolution principles change what I see on the page?" The builder has identified conflicts. Now the synthesis principles -- not just the conflict descriptions, but the resolution frameworks -- are applied. The question is not "how do I fix this conflict?" but "how does the principle that resolves this conflict change what I see EVERYWHERE?" Each resolution should propagate -- if density-organization interaction works THIS way in Zone 3, does it work the same way in Zone 7?

**Passes 7-10 (Framework Revision):** "Has my FRAMEWORK for evaluating the page changed, or have I just resolved a few specific conflicts? The synthesis should change how I think about density-organization interaction EVERYWHERE, not just where I found a conflict." This is the deepest phase. The builder is not resolving individual conflicts but asking whether the ACT of resolving conflicts has changed their evaluative framework. If the framework has not changed, the builder is applying synthesis at the wrong level.

## Wave-Specific Staleness Signatures

Staleness at Wave 4 has characteristic forms that differ from earlier waves. The 40+ passes of accumulated habituation make Wave 4 uniquely vulnerable:

**Resolving specific conflicts without revising the evaluative framework.** The most common Wave 4 failure. You find 3 conflicts, resolve them, and feel done. But the synthesis was supposed to change HOW YOU EVALUATE, not just fix specific problems. If your framework for evaluating density-organization interaction is the same as before reading the synthesis, you resolved symptoms.

**Confirming existing decisions.** Reading synthesis as validation rather than challenge. The synthesis says "density and organization interact" and you think "yes, my page already does that." But the synthesis is not confirming your work -- it is offering a framework for finding where your work FAILS. If you read the synthesis and everything on your page looks right, you are reading for confirmation.

**Habituation blindness.** 40+ passes means jarring inconsistencies have become "normal." You have seen the page so many times that contradictions between density and organization no longer register as contradictions. The synthesis documents offer a fresh vocabulary for problems you have stopped noticing. Are you using that vocabulary to see freshly, or are you translating it into your existing vocabulary and confirming what you already believe?

**Meta-evaluation staying shallow.** Evaluation stays at the "what conflicts exist?" level without reaching "how has my framework for evaluation changed?" Identifying conflicts is pass 1-3 work. By pass 7, the question should be about whether your entire evaluative apparatus has shifted.

**Defending decisions out of attachment.** When challenged by synthesis principles, the builder defends Wave 2-3 decisions because they were hard-won, not because they are right. The test: can you construct a compelling counter-argument against your proudest work? If you cannot, the defense may be attachment rather than quality.

**Never constructing counter-arguments.** Every notebook entry about synthesis confirms existing decisions. Zero entries argue against the builder's own work. The absence of counter-arguments is a staleness signal -- it means the synthesis is being used as a mirror rather than a lens.

## Wave-Specific Retrospective Questions

At the end of Wave 4, the builder asks questions that could NOT be asked before the synthesis was absorbed:

1. **"What conflicts did I discover between my Wave 2 and Wave 3 applications? How did resolving them change my understanding?"** This measures whether the synthesis was generative or perfunctory. The answer should name specific CSS decisions that contradicted each other and describe how resolution changed the builder's understanding of the interaction between dimensions.

2. **"Has the synthesis changed my FRAMEWORK for evaluating, or have I just resolved individual conflicts?"** This is the deepest question. The answer should articulate the framework shift -- not "I fixed the density-organization conflict in Zone 3" but "I now understand that density and organization are not independent dimensions but aspects of a single rhythm, and I evaluate them together everywhere."

3. **"Can I argue against my proudest design decision using synthesis principles? What does the counter-argument reveal?"** This tests synthesis depth. The builder should be able to construct a compelling argument against their best work -- and the counter-argument should reveal something about the tension between principles that the original decision resolved.

4. **"What dimensional interactions was I previously blind to that synthesis made visible?"** This measures whether the synthesis expanded perception. The answer should name specific interactions -- "I did not see that typographic rhythm and density were fighting in Zone 5 until the synthesis made me evaluate them together."

5. **"Am I evaluating at a higher level than Wave 3, or am I applying the same evaluation with different reference material?"** This is the meta-evaluation question. If the answer is "same evaluation, different material," the synthesis has not landed. The evaluation at Wave 4 should be ABOUT evaluation at Waves 1-3 -- a level up, not a lateral move.

## Cognitive Transformation Gate

The gate tests whether the builder's FRAMEWORK for evaluating the page has changed. Not whether specific conflicts were resolved -- whether the act of resolving them produced a new way of seeing.

### The Five Gate Conditions

1. **Has the builder's FRAMEWORK for evaluating the page changed?** Not "were conflicts resolved?" but "does the builder evaluate density-organization interaction differently NOW than before the synthesis?" The framework shift should be articulable -- the builder should be able to describe what they evaluate differently and why.

2. **Can the builder articulate how density-organization interaction works EVERYWHERE on the page?** Not just where they found conflicts -- everywhere. The synthesis should have produced a general understanding of dimensional interaction, not a local patch for specific problems.

3. **Has the builder constructed compelling counter-arguments against their proudest decisions?** This proves the synthesis was absorbed as a lens, not used as a checklist. The counter-arguments should be genuinely compelling -- not straw men set up to be knocked down.

4. **Have synthesis principles changed what the builder SEES?** Not just what they think about what they see -- what they PERCEIVE. After synthesis, the builder should see interactions between dimensions they were previously treating as separate. The perceptual shift should be demonstrable: "Before synthesis, I saw Zone 5 as a density problem. After synthesis, I see it as a density-typography entanglement."

5. **Does the 3-condition exhaustion model hold?**
   - **Delta stabilized:** Conflicts resolved. The last 2-3 passes produced refinements to resolutions, not new conflict discoveries.
   - **Research re-read produces no new interactions:** The synthesis re-read produces no new dimensional interactions that the builder has not already addressed.
   - **Infinite time answer is invisible:** Every remaining proposed change is below the perceptual threshold.

   All three conditions must hold simultaneously.

### What the Gate Is NOT

The minimum pass count (8) serves as anti-compression insurance ONLY. It is not the gate. A builder who reaches pass 8 and has not experienced framework revision should continue. A builder who reaches pass 12 and meets all five conditions should transition. The gate is cognitive transformation -- specifically, transformation of the evaluative framework -- not a counter.

Resolving all identified conflicts is necessary but not sufficient. The gate tests whether the ACT of resolution changed the builder's framework, not whether the conflicts are gone.

## Definition Journal Entry

At the START of Wave 4, write in the notebook: **"My current definition of 'good' for this page is: ___."**

At the END of Wave 4, write it again. Compare. The sentences should be qualitatively different -- not "more dimensions addressed" but "a fundamentally different way of thinking about what makes the page work."

Example evolution:
- Start of Wave 4: "Good = the page has strong density, clear organization, and rich case-study-informed spatial forms"
- End of Wave 4: "Good = every element operates at the intersection of multiple dimensions simultaneously AND removing any one dimension would visibly damage the others -- the page is a system of entangled interactions, not a collection of independently good treatments"

If the end-of-wave definition is about individual dimensions rather than their interactions, the synthesis has not landed. The shift from "good dimensions" to "good interactions between dimensions" IS the Wave 4 transformation.
