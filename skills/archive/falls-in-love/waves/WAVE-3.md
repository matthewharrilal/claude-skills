## The Fundamental Shift: From Findings to Spatial Explorations

Wave 2 absorbed RESEARCH FINDINGS -- discrete observations about spatial design, each a claim or principle. Wave 3 absorbs something fundamentally different: CASE STUDIES -- complete spatial explorations showing how other builders discovered spatial forms from content. The absorption mechanics change accordingly.

Each case study is a **complete spatial exploration with anti-prescription architecture**. They are NOT finding sets. They are reference works showing how other builders discovered spatial forms from content. Read as PROCESSES to study, not outputs to copy.

The difference is the difference between studying a painter's TECHNIQUE and copying their painting. The technique is transferable -- the way they build up layers, the way they handle transitions between areas of focus, the way they resolve competing visual demands. The painting is not transferable -- it is their response to their subject, not yours. A case study's Section 4 "collapse moment" (where a single spatial metaphor resolves multiple design problems) shows you what collapse LOOKS LIKE so you can recognize it in your own work. It does not give you a collapse to deploy.

### How to Engage with the Anti-Prescription Architecture

Each case study begins with Section 1: "NOT A TEMPLATE." This is not a disclaimer. It is a cognitive frame-setting instruction. When you read "NOT A TEMPLATE," your absorption mode should shift from "what can I take from this?" to "what PROCESS can I study here?" The entire case study that follows is a worked example of spatial discovery -- someone else's journey from content to form. Your job is to study the journey, not replicate the destination.

The anti-prescription architecture has a specific structure. Engage with each section for what it teaches about PROCESS:

**How to read each case study:**
1. **Section 1 ("NOT A TEMPLATE"):** Read first. Set the cognitive frame: this is a specific exploration, not a pattern library.
2. **Section 3 (Tension Derivation):** Study the PROCESS of deriving spatial tension from content. This is the most transferable content.
3. **Section 4 (The Search in Action):** Study the **collapse moment** -- where a single spatial metaphor resolves multiple design problems simultaneously. This is what your OWN spatial discovery should feel like.
4. **Section 5 (Mechanisms vs. Metaphor):** Separate **reusable mechanisms** (e.g., "Dense/Sparse Alternation") from **metaphor-specific implementations** (e.g., "4-level fractal structure"). Extract only the reusable. Translate the metaphor-specific.
5. **Section 6 (Divergence Assignment):** Complete the divergence verification for any case study whose spatial approach resembles your own. The 5-dimension table is your pattern-matching detector.

### Studying the Collapse Moment (Section 4)

The collapse moment is the instant where a single spatial metaphor resolves multiple design problems simultaneously. In DD-006, the fractal metaphor collapses density, hierarchy, and self-similarity into one spatial logic. In CD-006, the pilot migration metaphor collapses organizational hierarchy, reading path variation, and compound dimensionality into one spatial argument.

What does this feel like? It feels like several separate design problems you have been solving independently suddenly reveal themselves as ONE problem with ONE spatial answer. If you have been wrestling with "how to show hierarchy" AND "how to pace density" AND "how to vary reading direction" as three separate questions, the collapse moment is when you discover that a single content-derived metaphor answers all three. You stop solving three problems and start enacting one vision.

Study Section 4 in each case study to learn what collapse looks like from outside. Then watch for it in your own building.

### Separating Reusable from Metaphor-Specific (Section 5)

Section 5 of each case study distinguishes reusable mechanisms from metaphor-specific implementations. The reusable is vocabulary -- spatial patterns that work across content. "Dense/Sparse Alternation" is vocabulary. "Border-weight gradient encoding hierarchy" is vocabulary. These are words you can speak in your own spatial sentences.

The metaphor-specific is someone else's poem in that vocabulary. "4-level fractal structure" is DD-006's poem. "Pilot migration corridor" is CD-006's poem. You cannot borrow someone else's poem. You can learn their vocabulary and write your own.

The swap test works as an absorption diagnostic here: if a borrowed component works on your page without modification, you copied a poem rather than learned vocabulary. Every borrowed component should FAIL the swap test -- meaning it has been translated into your content's language, not transplanted from someone else's.

### Reading Your Own Page as a Case Study

By pass 10+, your page is itself a spatial exploration worth studying. Read your own HTML the way you read DD-006 or CD-006:
- What is the "collapse moment" in your own work -- where did a single spatial idea resolve multiple design problems?
- What mechanisms did you use, and which are content-specific vs. reusable?
- Where are the Section 5 boundaries in your own work -- what is vocabulary and what is your poem?
- If someone studied your page as a case study, what PROCESS would they extract?

This self-study is not narcissism. It is the point where case study absorption loops back into your own building and the distinction between "studying others" and "deepening your own work" dissolves.

## Dimension Visibility at Wave 3

Wave 3 marks a critical expansion in dimensional visibility. Case studies make ALL dimensions visible through worked examples -- not as abstract principles (Wave 2) but as concrete spatial decisions with traced reasoning chains.

**Structural** deepens into SPATIAL ARCHITECTURE. Case studies show structural decisions as interpretive choices, not layout defaults. DD-006's fractal structure is not "a layout" -- it is an interpretation of the content's self-similar argument. When you read a case study's structural decisions, ask: "What interpretive claim is this structure making about the content?" Your own structural decisions should make equally specific claims.

**Temporal** deepens. DD case studies show density as temporal pacing -- dense zones feel fast, sparse zones feel slow. The pacing is not arbitrary but content-derived: the content accelerates (lists, commands, rapid examples) and decelerates (philosophy, reflection, synthesis), and the density rhythm tracks this velocity. Your temporal dimension should now encode your content's specific velocity profile, not generic "some sections dense, some sparse."

**Material** deepens. Border-weight hierarchies, background surface gradients, and padding compression become semantic encoders -- not decorative treatments. OD case studies show how material quality differentiates organizational zones: the institutional zone feels like concrete (heavy borders, tight spacing, dense line-height) while the personal zone feels like air (thin borders, open spacing, breathing line-height). Your material dimension should encode your content's register shifts.

**Chromatic** may ACTIVATE if case studies demonstrate semantic color usage beyond the base palette. Before Wave 3, chromatic was dormant -- the base palette was sufficient. If case studies show how color events mark content transitions (desaturation for authority, first hue for liberation, re-desaturation for warning), the chromatic dimension wakes up. But only if YOUR content demands it. Chromatic activation without content demand is decoration.

**Typographic** deepens into CHARACTER-LEVEL encoding. DD-006 shows letter-spacing as semantic encoding -- not "tight tracking for headings" but letter-spacing that varies by content register, encoding density at the character level. Your typographic dimension should now operate at section, component, AND character scales simultaneously.

**Behavioral** deepens. Reading paths (from Axis work, derived from R4 + CD-006) ARE the behavioral dimension. The reader's eye movement through the page is a behavior -- and case studies show how that behavior can be content-derived rather than convention-driven.

**Relational** FULLY ACTIVATES. R5 combination theory (absorbed in Wave 2) described how components relate in principle. CD case studies show it in practice -- sequential vs. simultaneous deployment, transition grammar between axis changes, moments where the WHOLE exceeds the SUM OF PARTS. Your page's components should now argue with each other across distance, not just sit in their sections.

**Atmospheric** BEGINS TO EMERGE. When enough dimensions are active, the gestalt starts to form. You cannot build atmosphere directly. You can build structure + temporal pacing + material quality + chromatic arc + typographic voice + behavioral paths + relational arguments, and when they are all working in concert, the page starts to FEEL like something. Atmosphere is the emergent property. Watch for it. If it does not emerge, one or more dimensions are underserving the content.

**Key: case studies show dimensions in COMPOUND interaction, not isolation.** DD-006 does not demonstrate "density" in a vacuum -- it demonstrates density interacting with hierarchy interacting with self-similarity. This is where entanglement becomes visible. A single case study teaches you more about dimensional interaction than all of R1-R5 combined, because the interaction is ENACTED, not described.

## Content Reading Depth at Wave 3

The builder moves from **IMPLIES** to **DEMANDS** during this wave.

**IMPLIES deepening:** In Wave 2, the builder began seeing what the content implies beyond what it says. Case studies deepen this capacity dramatically. "The content implies a hierarchy" is a Wave 2 observation. Case studies show HOW other builders encoded implied hierarchies -- the specific CSS decisions, the interpretive chains, the collapse moments. After studying DD-006's fractal hierarchy and OD-004's confidence gradient, you can see MORE implications in your own content. The case studies give you new eyes. Your content has not changed. Your capacity to perceive its implications has.

**DEMANDS emerging:** DEMANDS is a qualitative shift from IMPLIES. IMPLIES says "this content suggests a spatial form." DEMANDS says "this content REQUIRES a spatial form -- without it, the content is actively damaged." The difference: "The content implies a culmination signal" vs. "The content DEMANDS a culmination signal -- it arrives at a conclusion that must be spatially amplified, and failing to amplify it is a betrayal of the content's argument."

Case studies are what make DEMANDS visible. Before seeing how CD-006 handled its content's demands, you could not recognize your own content's demands. Before seeing how OD-004 spatially amplified its content's confidence gradient, you could not feel where your own content INSISTS on amplification. DEMANDS is not stronger IMPLIES. It is a different cognitive state -- the recognition that certain spatial forms are non-negotiable because the content will not survive without them.

**The reciprocal relationship deepens:** Studying other builders' responses to THEIR content's demands helps you hear YOUR content's demands more clearly. DD-006's builder responded to their content's demand for self-similarity at every scale. OD-004's builder responded to their content's demand for a visible confidence trajectory. Seeing their responses does not tell you what YOUR content demands. It trains your ear to hear demands. After studying 3-4 case studies' demand-response patterns, you start hearing your own content speak with a specificity it did not have before.

## How the Cycle Evolves Across 10+ Passes Per Dimension

Each pass is a full build-look-refine cycle (same as every wave -- this is always true). Read the case study --> build CSS changes --> screenshot and scroll --> evaluate --> refine --> note what you learned. Each cycle, the case study means something different because you have built more page.

**The notebook never mentions a case study dimension outside its primacy pass.** DD is mentioned only during DD primacy passes. OD disappears when OD is not at primacy. This means dimension permeation is not happening -- you are treating dimensions as discrete tasks rather than continuous lenses.

**Zero cross-dimension prompts.** No thinking about how DD interacts with OD interacts with CD. Each dimension is absorbed in isolation. ## Cognitive Transformation Gate

The gate tests whether studying other builders' processes has changed how the builder sees their OWN content -- not just their page.

### The Six Gate Conditions

1. **Has studying other builders' processes changed how the builder sees their OWN content?** Not just "I learned techniques from DD-006." The question is: "After studying DD-006's process for deriving self-similarity from content, I now SEE self-similarity in my own content that I could not see before." The case studies changed the builder's perceptual capacity, not just their spatial vocabulary.

2. **Does every borrowed component fail the swap test?** This is evidence of translation, not copying. If a component was inspired by a case study and works on your page without modification, it was copied. Every borrowed component should be so content-specific that transplanting it to different content would break it. This is the binary test for whether absorption happened at the process level or the output level.

3. **Can the builder describe compound effects where DD + OD produce something neither alone could?** This proves dimensional interaction, not just dimensional presence. The builder should be able to point to specific page elements where two or more dimensions interact to create an effect that removing either dimension would destroy.

4. **Has the builder completed Section 6 divergence verification for any similar approaches?** If any case study's spatial approach resembles the builder's approach, the 5-dimension divergence table should have been completed. This is the pattern-matching detector that prevents unconscious copying.

5. **Has case study absorption deepened content reading from IMPLIES to DEMANDS?** The builder should be able to name specific content features that DEMAND spatial expression -- not "suggest" or "could benefit from" but DEMAND. These demands should be qualitatively different from the implications identified at Wave 2. If the builder's content reading has not shifted from "this content implies" to "this content demands," the case studies did not deepen perception.

6. **The mechanism catalog re-read reveals no un-applied mechanisms that serve this content.** Re-read mechanisms.md and grammar.md at wave end. For every mechanism, the builder can say either "applied" (it is in the page), "irrelevant to this content" (with specific reason), or "addressed indirectly" (the mechanism's intent is served by a different spatial choice). If any mechanism that serves this content remains un-applied without reason, the wave is not exhausted.

### The 3-Condition Exhaustion Model at Wave 3

The universal exhaustion model applies: (1) Delta stabilized -- case-study-driven CSS changes produce diminishing returns. (2) Research re-read produces no new spatial ideas -- re-reading case studies and the mechanism catalog reveals no un-applied mechanisms that serve this content. (3) The "infinite time" answer is invisible-grain adjustments to compound interactions, not new spatial additions. These three conditions are the CORE of the gate. The six conditions above are diagnostic evidence that the exhaustion model holds.

### What the Gate Is NOT

The minimum pass count (12) serves as anti-compression insurance ONLY. It is not the gate. Extend to 18 if dimensions remain under-engaged -- if any dimension has fewer than 3 active build-look-refine cycles across the wave, it is under-engaged and needs more passes. A builder who reaches pass 12 and has not experienced cognitive transformation should continue. A builder who reaches pass 18 and meets all six conditions should transition.

CSS line counts are not the gate. Screenshot counts are not the gate. The gate is cognitive transformation -- specifically, the transformation from reading case studies as instruction sets to thinking in the vocabulary those case studies demonstrated.
