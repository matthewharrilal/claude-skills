# Wave 7: Self-Audit Cycle (8-12 passes)

**Input:** The page as built through Waves 0-6. The original content. The builder's notebook. The 10 falsifiable criteria. No new external files.

The agent evaluates its own work as if encountering it for the first time.

---

## What This Wave Is

**Every pass in Wave 7 is still a full build-look-refine cycle.** The audit IS the cycle -- not a separate activity. Pass 1 is not "take screenshots." Pass 1 is: take screenshots -> evaluate what you see -> build changes based on the evaluation -> screenshot again -> refine -> decide what next. The named activities below (adversarial, criteria, comprehensive, content-coupling, surgery) describe the EVALUATION LENS for each pass's cycle, not the entire pass.

Wave 7 is the wave where the builder absorbs their OWN WORK as if it were a foreign artifact. After 80+ passes of building, the builder knows every pixel of the page. That familiarity is both the builder's greatest asset and their greatest liability. The asset: nobody understands this page's spatial argument better than the builder who spent 80+ passes constructing it. The liability: the builder has stopped SEEING the page. Familiarity has replaced perception. The page has become wallpaper.

The cognitive challenge of Wave 7 is achieving genuine fresh eyes after 80+ passes. This requires deliberate mode-switching -- not just "look at the page again" but "look at the page AS IF you are a different person with a different agenda." Mode-switching (experiential -> analytical -> adversarial -> content-coupling -> responsive -> architectural) disrupts habitual perception. Each mode forces the builder to see the page through a lens that conflicts with the lens they used while building. The adversarial pass does not ask "is this good?" -- it asks "what would someone who thinks my approach is WRONG see?" That question requires imagining a specific critic, not a generic one. Imagine a designer who thinks your spatial approach is fundamentally misguided, not just imperfect. What do they see that you cannot?

The gravity of confirmation bias at this depth cannot be overstated. The builder has invested 80+ passes and has enormous incentive to pass the audit. Every evaluation instinct tilts toward "this is good" rather than "this is wrong." Every pass of Wave 7 must fight the gravity of self-congratulation. Seek what is WRONG, not what is RIGHT.

---

## The Self-Audit Passes

**Pass 1:** Full-page screenshots at all 3 viewports. One complete scroll-through WITHOUT analyzing -- just experiencing. The experiential scroll-through should feel DIFFERENT from the scroll-throughs of Waves 2-6. After 80+ passes, the builder's perception is saturated. The question is not "what am I seeing?" but "what am I seeing that I have STOPPED seeing because I have seen it too many times?" Let the scroll happen at reading speed. Do not stop to examine. Let discomfort arrive on its own -- after this many passes, problems are felt before they are understood. If nothing feels uncomfortable, that is itself a signal: either the page is genuinely excellent, or your perception has been anesthetized by familiarity. Assume the latter until the adversarial pass proves otherwise.

**Pass 2:** The adversarial pass. "What would someone who disagrees with my approach say? What would a designer with 20 years of experience notice that I am missing? What would break if I removed my cleverest spatial invention?" The adversarial pass requires genuine vulnerability, not theater. The technique: imagine a specific critic who thinks your spatial approach is WRONG -- not a generic "tough reviewer" but a designer who would choose a fundamentally different spatial strategy for this content. What would they build instead? What do they see in your page that confirms their skepticism? If the adversarial pass produces zero genuine vulnerabilities, you are performing adversarial review rather than conducting it. Every approach has weaknesses. Finding none means you are not looking.

**Pass 3-4:** Apply the 10 falsifiable criteria (see Measurement below). Score honestly. At this depth -- after 80+ passes -- the 10 falsifiable criteria should feel like a FLOOR, not a ceiling. If they still feel like stretch goals, the build is not deep enough. If they feel trivially met, the builder's sense of quality has surpassed the criteria -- which is the POINT. The criteria exist to set a minimum, not to define excellence. The question at this depth is not "do I pass the criteria?" but "what does excellence look like BEYOND these criteria, for THIS content?"

**Pass 5-6:** The comprehensive pass. Check every zone transition, every density oscillation, every typography relationship, every responsive behavior, every hover state. This is the last time individual dimensions are evaluated in isolation. After this pass, everything is atmospheric -- the question shifts from "is each dimension working?" to "which dimensional interaction is the weakest link in the atmospheric experience?"

**Pass 7-8:** The content-coupling pass. Re-read the content one final time. Does EVERY spatial decision serve the content's meaning? Where is the form still generic? This pass tests whether content-form fusion is genuine. Re-read the content not to discover new spatial potential (that was Waves 0-6) but to verify that EVERY spatial decision serves content meaning. Where is the form still ADEQUATE when it should be INEVITABLE? Where is the layout still convention (a reasonable way to present this content) rather than derivation (the ONLY way to present this content)? For every section where the form is adequate, ask: "What specific content feature is the form not yet serving? What would make this INEVITABLE?"

**Pass 9-12:** Final surgery based on everything the audit revealed. Each fix must arise from the audit findings, not from habitual tendencies. Ask: "Am I fixing what the audit found, or what I always fix?" Surgery should be targeted to specific findings, not reflexive tinkering. If the surgery addresses a spacing issue, ask why the spacing felt wrong -- fix the cause, not the symptom.

## How to Distinguish Audit-Driven Fixes from Habitual Tendencies

After 80+ passes, the builder has developed patterns -- things they always check, things they always adjust, things they feel nervous about. These habitual tendencies are not the same as audit findings. An audit finding is: "The adversarial pass revealed that the transition between sections 4 and 5 lacks material differentiation -- the chromatic and border-weight shifts that mark every other major transition are absent here." A habitual tendency is: "I always feel like the spacing could be tighter in the middle section."

The test: can you trace the fix back to a specific audit pass and a specific finding? If yes, it is audit-driven surgery. If no -- if the fix is something you "just feel" should be changed -- interrogate it. Is this genuine taste refined by 80+ passes? Or is it the builder's reflexive desire to keep building past the point of usefulness? Both exist at this depth. Distinguishing them requires honesty the builder may not want to exercise.

## Dimension Visibility at Wave 7

All 8 dimensions are fully active, but perception has INVERTED. The question is no longer "is each dimension working?" -- the question is "which dimensional interaction is the weakest link in the atmospheric experience?"

### The Mode-Switching Protocol

Each evaluation mode in the pass sequence evaluates different dimensional combinations:

**Experiential mode (Pass 1):** Evaluates atmospheric wholeness. All 8 dimensions at once, felt rather than analyzed. The question is: "What does the page FEEL like to encounter as a stranger?" This is the mode that detects atmospheric failures -- moments where the totality breaks, where the page stops feeling like a unified experience and starts feeling like assembled parts. After 80+ passes, problems are felt before they are understood. Trust the discomfort.

**Adversarial mode (Pass 2):** Looks for dimensional conflicts -- places where two dimensions argue against each other rather than reinforcing each other. The adversarial critic sees the seams. Where does the chromatic arc contradict the material progression? Where does the typographic voice shift without the temporal pacing shifting to match? Dimensional conflicts are invisible to the builder who constructed both dimensions at different times. The adversarial mode makes them visible by asking "what is WRONG with how these dimensions interact?"

**Analytical mode (Passes 3-4):** Evaluates individual dimensions against the 10 falsifiable criteria. This is the last moment of isolated dimensional evaluation. Each dimension is measured on its own terms. Is the structural dimension serving content topology? Is the temporal dimension encoding the content's emotional arc? Is the chromatic dimension using color as event?

**Comprehensive mode (Passes 5-6):** Evaluates dimensional entanglement at fine grain. Every zone transition, every density oscillation, every hover state. The question is not "does dimension X work?" but "at THIS specific moment in the page, are ALL dimensions working together?" Walk through the page zone by zone, checking that material + typographic + chromatic + temporal all serve the same content moment.

**Content-coupling mode (Passes 7-8):** Evaluates dimensions through DISCOMFORT, not analysis. After 80+ passes, the builder reads the content one final time and asks: "Where does the dimensional expression feel ADEQUATE rather than INEVITABLE? Where could a different builder, with a different spatial approach, achieve the same dimensional expression?" Adequacy is the enemy. Inevitability is the standard.

**Surgical mode (Passes 9-12):** No longer evaluating dimensions. Fixing specific dimensional interactions identified in previous passes. Each fix is traced to a specific finding from a specific pass.

## Content Reading Depth at Wave 7

The builder is in deep **FUSES** (Stage 5), with the reciprocal relationship between content and form at maximum intensity.

The content-coupling pass (passes 7-8) tests whether FUSES is genuine. Re-read the content one final time. This re-reading should feel DIFFERENT from Wave 0. The builder has built 80+ passes. The content should reveal features that were literally invisible before building began. If the content reads the same as it did at Wave 0, the fusion is superficial -- the builder has been applying form TO content rather than deriving form FROM content.

The ultimate FUSES test: "Does the page make me notice content features I had not noticed before?" If the form is truly content-derived, it should TEACH the builder about the content even at this stage. The spatial interpretation should illuminate the text the way a good literary essay illuminates the novel it analyzes. If the page is genuinely fused with the content, looking at the page and then reading the content should produce a DIFFERENT reading experience than reading the content alone. The page is not an illustration of the content. It is an interpretation that reveals something the text alone does not show.

The content-coupling audit differs from content reading at earlier waves. You are not reading the content for new understanding (that was Waves 0-6). You are reading it to check whether EVERY spatial decision serves content meaning. The question has shifted from "what does the content want to become?" to "has the page become what the content demanded?" Where is the form still GENERIC (layout convention applied to this content) rather than INEVITABLE (form derived from this content's specific meaning)?

## Pass-Level Deepening Within Wave 7

**Passes 1-2 (Experiential + Adversarial):** Evaluation asks "what does the page FEEL like to encounter? What would a hostile critic see?" (fresh perception through mode-switching). These passes establish the audit's agenda. The experiential pass discovers what feels wrong. The adversarial pass discovers what IS wrong that the builder cannot feel because familiarity has numbed perception.

**Passes 3-6 (Criteria + Comprehensive):** Evaluation asks "where does the page fail on measurable criteria AND where does it succeed on criteria but fail on taste?" (the gap between measurable and felt quality). This is the critical distinction of deep building: a page can pass every criterion and still not be GOOD. The criteria measure the floor. Taste -- refined by 80+ passes of building -- measures the ceiling. Where the page passes criteria but fails taste, there is work to do. Where it fails criteria, the work is obvious. The subtle failures are where it passes criteria and the builder has stopped asking whether it is good enough.

**Passes 7-8 (Content-Coupling):** Evaluation asks "for every section where the form is ADEQUATE, what would make it INEVITABLE? What specific content feature is the form not yet serving?" (pushing from good to content-derived). The content-coupling pass is the final test of the build's depth. Every section that is merely adequate -- where the form is reasonable but not necessary -- is a section where the builder's absorption stopped short. What content feature in that section has no spatial expression? What would the form look like if it served THAT feature?

**Passes 9-12 (Surgery):** Evaluation asks "does each fix arise from the audit or from my habitual tendencies? Am I fixing what the audit found or what I always fix?" (surgical precision vs. default behavior). Surgery at this depth requires discipline. The temptation is to keep refining -- to adjust spacing, tune typography, shift colors -- because the builder has been building for hours and the building itself has become habitual. Each fix must be traceable to a finding. If it is not traceable, it is habitual, and habitual fixes at this depth are as likely to degrade the page as improve it.

## Staleness Signatures for Wave 7

These are the specific ways staleness manifests during self-audit. If you detect two or more, STOP and apply the staleness recovery protocol from SKILL.md.

1. **CONFIRMATION BIAS:** Looking at the page to verify it is good, not to discover it is not. The notebook entries read like a defense of the page rather than an interrogation of it. Every observation ends with "which works well" or "as intended."

2. **Zero genuine vulnerabilities from the adversarial pass.** The adversarial pass producing zero findings means you are not looking. Every approach has weaknesses -- every spatial strategy makes tradeoffs. Finding none means the adversarial mode was performed rather than inhabited. Imagine a designer who thinks your entire spatial approach is wrong. What do they see?

3. **Audit findings clustering in comfortable categories.** All findings are about typography and spacing. None are about spatial argument, content coupling, or dimensional interaction. The builder audits what they are comfortable evaluating and avoids what they are not. If every finding is at the CSS-property level ("this margin should be larger") and none are at the spatial-argument level ("this section's form does not serve its content"), the audit is shallow.

4. **Surgery addressing symptoms instead of causes.** Fixing a spacing issue without asking WHY the spacing felt wrong. The spacing may be a symptom of a structural problem -- the section's grid ratio does not serve its content, and the spacing awkwardness is the downstream effect. Symptom-fixing produces passes of changes that do not improve the page because the cause persists.

5. **The pride question producing defensive answers.** When asked "what is the weakest part of this page?", the builder answers with qualifications: "Well, given the constraints..." or "It could be better but..." instead of directly naming the weakness. Defensive answers are evidence that the builder is protecting their investment rather than evaluating their work.

6. **Mode-switching failing.** Using the same evaluation lens for every pass instead of genuinely switching perspectives. If the experiential pass, the adversarial pass, and the comprehensive pass all produce the same observations, the builder is not actually switching modes -- they are running the same evaluation three times with different labels.

7. **Zero surprising findings.** If the audit surprises you nowhere, you are seeing what you expect, not what is there. After 80+ passes, the builder has a detailed mental model of the page. Genuine fresh-eyes evaluation should produce at least one finding that contradicts that mental model. If every finding confirms what you already believed, the audit is confirmatory, not evaluative.

## Wave-Specific Retrospective Questions

At the end of Wave 7, write a retrospective addressing the base questions from SKILL.md PLUS these wave-specific questions:

1. **"What did the adversarial pass reveal that I was avoiding? What is the genuine weakness in my approach?"** -- Name it directly. Not "there are some areas that could be improved" but "the spatial argument in section 5 is weak because the form does not serve the content's shift from institutional to personal register." If you cannot name a genuine weakness, the adversarial pass was theater.

2. **"Do the 10 falsifiable criteria feel like a floor or a ceiling? If a ceiling, why?"** -- If the criteria still feel like stretch goals at pass 80+, investigate why. Either the build is not deep enough, or the criteria are measuring something the build has already surpassed. Both are important to understand.

3. **"For every section where the form is ADEQUATE, what would make it INEVITABLE?"** -- Name the specific content feature that the form is not yet serving. Adequacy is "a reasonable way to present this content." Inevitability is "the ONLY way to present this content, derived from the content's specific meaning." The gap between these two is the audit's most important finding.

4. **"What would a stranger notice in the first 5 seconds that I can no longer see?"** -- The familiarity blindspot. After 80+ passes, the builder has lost the ability to see the page as a stranger sees it. What is the first impression? What dominates? What is invisible? The experiential scroll-through at pass 1 attempted to recover this perspective. Did it succeed?

5. **"Can I name what this page achieves that could NOT have been achieved in 20 passes? What is the product of the additional 60+ passes?"** -- This is the deep-build proof. If the page could have been built in 20 passes, the remaining 60+ were not deepening -- they were tinkering. The builder should be able to point to specific spatial decisions, dimensional interactions, and content-coupling moments that required the accumulated context of 60+ passes to become visible. If they cannot, the build plateaued and the additional passes did not produce cognitive deepening.

6. **"Did the audit change my assessment of the page, or did it confirm what I already believed?"** -- If the audit only confirmed prior beliefs, it was not an audit. A genuine audit produces findings that the builder did not expect. The audit should have changed something about how the builder sees the page -- even if the change is small. If nothing changed, the builder was seeking confirmation, not evaluation.

## Cognitive Transformation Gate

Wave 7 is exhausted when ALL of the following are true:

### The Three Universal Exhaustion Conditions

1. **The delta profile has stabilized at invisible grain.** The last 2-3 passes produced surgical fixes traced to specific audit findings. Changes are below the perceptual threshold for casual observation.
2. **The audit re-evaluation produces no new findings.** Re-running any evaluation mode (experiential, adversarial, analytical, comprehensive, content-coupling) does not reveal issues not already addressed.
3. **The "infinite time" answer is invisible.** Every proposed change is below the perceptual threshold -- invisible-grain adjustments that a reader would never notice.

### Wave 7 Cognitive Conditions

4. **Has the adversarial pass produced genuine vulnerability?** Can the builder name the weakest aspect of their approach -- not a trivial CSS detail, but a genuine spatial or content-coupling weakness? If the builder cannot name a weakness, the adversarial pass did not achieve genuine fresh-eyes evaluation.

5. **Do the 10 falsifiable criteria feel like a floor?** The build has surpassed them. The builder's sense of quality operates at a level the criteria do not measure. If the criteria still feel like a ceiling -- if the builder is straining to meet them -- the build is not at Wave 7 depth.

6. **Can the builder name what the page achieves that could NOT have been achieved in 20 passes?** This is the deep-build proof. The builder must articulate the specific product of sustained engagement -- the spatial decisions, dimensional interactions, and content-coupling moments that required 60+ passes to become visible. If this cannot be articulated, the additional passes may not have produced genuine deepening.

7. **Has the content-coupling audit pushed sections from ADEQUATE toward INEVITABLE?** The builder can point to specific sections where the audit revealed that the form was generic rather than content-derived, and the surgery addressed that gap. Not all sections will reach INEVITABLE -- but the audit should have identified the gap and the surgery should have closed it where possible.

8. **Has the audit produced SURPRISING findings?** Evidence of genuine fresh-eyes evaluation. At least one finding should have contradicted the builder's prior assessment of the page. If every finding confirmed what the builder already believed, the audit was confirmatory rather than evaluative.

### The Exhaustion Model

Audit complete + fixes targeted to specific findings + builder can articulate what 60+ additional passes produced. The audit is not "done" when the builder has completed 8 passes. It is done when the builder has evaluated the page from multiple perspectives, found genuine issues, fixed them with surgical precision, and can articulate both the page's strengths and its remaining weaknesses with equal specificity.

### Minimum Pass Count

**Minimum passes: 8.** This is anti-compression insurance only. The gate is cognitive transformation, not a counter. A builder who reaches pass 8 without surprising findings should continue. A builder who reaches pass 12 and meets all conditions should transition. If the exhaustion conditions are met but the pass count has not reached 8, continue -- the minimum exists because self-audit that takes fewer than 8 passes is almost certainly superficial at this depth of build.
